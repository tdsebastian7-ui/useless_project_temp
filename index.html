01 / THE PROBLEM STATEMENT

The problem
that doesn't exist.

Family-group good morning images arrive with flowers, sunshine, and unwavering confidence. What they don't come with is evidence.

We wanted to know whether a daily greeting actually depicts a morning scene—and whether its visible weather resembles real conditions at a chosen place and date.

Someone had to ask. Apparently, it was us.

02 / THE SOLUTION

The solution
nobody asked for.

Good Morning Ji reads visual clues from your image and compares them with live or historical weather data from Open-Meteo.

Brightness and colour cues suggest an approximate photo time. Visual weather estimates are compared with recorded conditions, producing an explainable match score.

Less “nice pic, uncle.” More “let's examine the evidence.”

03 / THE PROCEDURE

Three steps. One unnecessary investigation.

  1. Submit the evidence.

    Upload a JPG, PNG, or WEBP morning image. Select the location and date you want to check.

  2. Check the sky.

    The app estimates visual conditions and approximate photo time, then retrieves real weather for your selection.

  3. Read the verdict.

    Compare the image prediction with recorded weather, inspect the match score, and optionally save the report.

04 / INSIDE THE BUREAU

Actual data. Questionable necessity.

What goes into a Good Morning Ji report
The evidenceWhat the app does with it
Light & colourUses brightness and colour balance to estimate approximate photo time.
Visual weatherEstimates cloud cover, rain likelihood, temperature, humidity, and visibility from image cues.
Real weatherRetrieves live or historical conditions through Open-Meteo, using your selected date and location.
A match scoreCompares the estimates with recorded weather and shows the comparison details.
Your case historySaves completed reports locally in your browser when you choose to save them.
IMPORTANT: A similarity check, not a scientific forecast.

Image-based weather and time estimates are uncertain. The local fallback uses simple Canvas colour heuristics—not a full understanding of the scene. A high match score does not prove that an image was taken at that place or time.

Does it need an AI API key?

The app works without an API key using a browser-based Canvas fallback. Developers can optionally configure a compatible vision-analysis endpoint with VITE_VISION_API_URL.

Where does my image go?

The fallback analyzes the image in your browser. If a remote vision endpoint is configured, the image is sent to that service for analysis. Saved report history stays in browser local storage.

What's under the hood?

React, TypeScript, Vite, HTML, CSS, the browser Canvas API, and Open-Meteo's Geocoding, Forecast, and Archive APIs. No hardware required. Just a morning worth investigating.
